# Drawing quality evaluation

Automated scene validation proves shape safety and consistency; it does not prove that a sketch resembles the photo. Complete this evaluation privately with an adult after configuring a backend key. No live photo evaluation has been performed yet.

## Ten representative non-personal photographs

Use your own photographs with plain backgrounds, no people, and one prominent subject. Do not commit the photographs or any credentials.

Score 1–5: 1 unusable, 3 acceptable, 5 excellent. Target ≥4 on every category. Also try crowded scenes, faces, unreadable photos, extreme lighting, and very complex objects; confirm a helpful request for a simpler non-personal photo.

For each accepted lesson:

1. Compare the finished drawing with the photo: preserve the silhouette, proportions, pose, and at least two distinguishing traits.
2. Follow all steps on physical paper with an adult. Record whether a seven-year-old can copy each group without unexplained leaps.
3. Confirm 4–6 steps, exact previous geometry, light older strokes, clear new strokes, and line-by-line reveal.
4. Inspect the full sketch at desktop and phone sizes, then print the worksheet. Look for cropped strokes, awkward joins, too much detail, unclear overlaps, and tiny instructions.
5. Reject technically valid but unrecognizable scenes. Tune prompts/models or request a simpler photo, and rerun all checks.

## Release work

Before releasing a children's product, assess parental consent and age controls, child-safety/content policy and moderation, provider data processing/retention, applicable privacy requirements, accessibility, abuse resistance, cost controls, and a larger diverse photo evaluation. This prototype makes no production child-safety or legal compliance claim.
